What can a business share after a volunteering day that helps someone understand the experience? For Collen Construction, the answer was a short film built around the people taking part, supported by two interviews and photographs. The company has already used **Collen 01 — Volunteers’ Experience on LinkedIn**. All three finished films are available on this page.

I filmed the project at **Malahide Marina on 13 November 2026**. Volunteers were counting oysters, preparing baskets and helping with their deployment beside the water. It was cold, people were moving between activities, and interviews had to fit around the day. The brief brought together [corporate event videography](/event-videographer-dublin/), photography and short business films with a clear place to be used.

## Why filming for Collen Construction meant a lot

Collen was established in **1810**. The [Voluntary Construction Register](https://www.voluntaryconstructionregister.ie/register/detail/collen-construction-ltd) describes it as one of Ireland’s oldest building firms. Its [company history](https://www.collen.com/about/our-history) traces more than two centuries of work and describes a business headquartered in Dublin, operating across Ireland, the UK and Europe.

Being trusted to make content for a company with that history was a privilege. The particular story I was there to cover belonged to the team at the marina: people giving their time, working together and explaining the experience in their own words. That gave the film something specific to say about the company’s people.

For a [corporate video](/corporate-video-production-dublin/), a recognisable client name gives context. The work still needs to show why the occasion matters. Here, that meant staying close to the volunteers and the activity, so someone watching afterwards could understand what the team had actually done.

## What was happening at Malahide Marina?

The event involved volunteers counting oysters into bundles, filling baskets and helping with deployment at the marina. The [Green Ocean Foundation’s Malahide project](https://greenoceanfoundation.ie/our-projects/malahide/) explains the wider work to restore native oyster populations in the estuary. That is the context around the activity shown in these films.

My coverage follows the part of the programme happening in front of the camera. You see people preparing the material, working beside the water and gathering together at the harbour. The images give the viewer a sequence to follow, from preparation to the activity itself, with the participants’ voices alongside it.

That distinction matters when a company commissions content about volunteering or sustainability. The film can show the day honestly and explain its purpose. Claims about longer-term environmental results need evidence from the organisation measuring that work. My role here was to document the event and the people involved.

## How do you turn one company event into LinkedIn content?

Begin with what a viewer needs to understand. For this project, the volunteers’ film introduces the day through people and activity. David and Rebecca’s interviews give individual accounts more room. The photographs offer moments that can be viewed on their own, including the group together beside the boats.

Those outputs have different uses. A short overview can introduce an event to someone scrolling through LinkedIn. An interview can give a person’s explanation more time. A photograph can accompany a written update. Planning that mix before filming helps a business commission material it can use after the event.

The delivered files include **16:9 landscape and 1:1 square versions of all three films**. Collen has used the main film on LinkedIn; the two interviews are presented here as additional finished work. For a similar brief, the [short-form video production service](/short-form-video-production-dublin/) covers how we agree the subjects, lengths and formats of the edits.

## Why keep the interviews as separate films?

An overview needs enough movement to explain the occasion. An interview needs enough time for a person to finish a useful thought. Keeping David and Rebecca’s films separate allowed their contributions to stand on their own, while the volunteers’ film could connect the people with the work happening around them.

Some interview opportunities appeared during the live event. People had tasks to get back to, and the day continued around us. I needed to find a useful moment for a conversation, listen to the answer and stay aware of what was happening nearby. That is a different rhythm from a booked interview in a quiet office.

If your team needs [interview-led corporate video](/corporate-video-production-dublin/), agree a few themes and identify who can speak about them before the event. Give each person time to explain something they know first-hand. A short answer about a real experience can be more useful than a broad statement about company values.

## What was difficult about filming in the cold?

At Malahide Marina, I felt the cold every time I stopped moving. My camera kept working through the conditions, and I kept following the event. The challenge was staying attentive: someone might begin filling a basket while another group gathered for a photograph or an interview became possible nearby.

I wanted the setting to remain visible in the work. The photographs show bright water, boats, red lifejackets and people wrapped up against the weather. Closer images show hands and concentration around the activity. Together, those details explain what being there looked like.

A useful [event filming plan](/event-videographer-dublin/) identifies the essential moments and leaves room for the day to unfold. For an outdoor programme, the location, access and practical working conditions should be discussed before agreeing the coverage. That preparation helps the filmmaker respond when something worth keeping happens unexpectedly.

## What did the drone footage add?

I also filmed with a drone. The wider view helps establish the marina and its relationship to the water. That gives the closer footage a setting: the viewer can understand where the volunteers are working before spending time with an individual person or a detail of the activity.

The personal part of the story comes from the ground-level coverage and interviews. Combining those perspectives gives the edit a way to move between the location, the group and an individual voice. The aerial material was one part of the coverage, alongside the photography and filming around the event.

For an event in [Malahide](/videographer-malahide/) or elsewhere in [North Dublin](/areas/), discuss aerial coverage when you share the brief. The actual location and conditions affect what can be planned. The production scope should make clear what is intended and what depends on the day.

## Can you get photographs and video from the same event?

This project delivered both. The group photograph records the people together at the marina. Other photographs focus on conversations, preparation and work beside the water. They follow the same subject as the films, so the client has material that belongs to one recognisable occasion.

Planning [event photography](/photography-dublin/) alongside video helps identify the moments that each format needs. A group portrait asks people to pause together; a film often needs them carrying on with the activity. Agreeing those needs in advance makes the coverage more practical for everyone involved.

Each event gives the finished work its own material and pace.

## What affects the cost of a corporate event video?

The quote needs to reflect the coverage and the finished work. For a project like this, useful questions include how long the filmmaker will be on location, how many people need interviewing, whether photographs are required, and how many separate edits and formats the client needs.

It also helps to nominate one person to gather feedback from the team. That gives the editor a clear set of changes and keeps the review practical when several people are involved.

Delivery timing also belongs in that conversation. Collen wanted content available promptly so the team could share the occasion while it was current. A brief should say when the first version is needed and who will review it. That allows the production and editing time to be planned around a real deadline.

## How could your business use a similar approach?

A volunteering day, team event or company gathering can provide a clear subject for a business film. Start with what happens, who is involved and what someone outside the event should understand. Then decide where the finished content will appear and which voices would help explain it.

For Collen, the result was a volunteers’ film used on LinkedIn, two individual interviews and photographs from Malahide Marina. The day gave us people, practical work and a place with its own character. My task was to bring those elements together in content the team could share.
