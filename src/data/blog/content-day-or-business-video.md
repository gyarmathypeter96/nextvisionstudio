Choose a Content Day when you need several short videos around your business, products or customer questions. Choose a Business Video when you need one clear explanation of your company or service for a website, proposal or sales conversation.

You can combine them when the filming plan allows it. If you are still deciding what the videos should do, read [how to plan a business video production](/blog/business-video-production-planning-dublin/) first. The finished video list must be agreed before the shoot, because a full website film and a batch of short social videos need different preparation and editing time.

## When does a Content Day make sense?

A Content Day is a planned filming session that creates footage for several agreed short videos. It suits a business preparing a campaign, refreshing a social profile or answering a group of common customer questions.

Before filming, choose the subjects and prepare talking points. Decide whether each video needs a person speaking, a demonstration, product footage or a combination. A quiet interview room and a busy customer area may need to be filmed at different times.

The [Leroy’s Barking World project](/case-studies/leroys-barking-world-social-content/) shows how scripts and a shot plan supported funny, educational videos alongside the owner’s own phone content. It is an example of the approach, not a fixed video quantity promised for every shoot.

## When is a Business Video the better starting point?

A main business film is useful when customers need help understanding your service or trusting the people behind it. It can introduce the team, explain a process or show a customer story where permission is available.

The filming plan follows that message. An interview may need supporting footage of the work being described. A product demonstration needs the product ready to use, and a customer story needs the right participant and an approved subject.

Our [VentSolve project](/case-studies/ventsolve-content-production/) includes public promotional videos as well as separate educational and internal content. That shows why the audience and purpose need to be clear before deciding what to film.

## What is included in a NextVision production proposal?

We bring planning, filming, editing and delivery into one written scope. Check these details before booking:

* The audience, message and topics to prepare.
* Filming time, locations and people needed on the day.
* The number and length of final videos.
* Vertical or horizontal versions, subtitles and any photography.
* First edit and final delivery dates, with the included changes.
* Where you can use the finished work and how files are handed over.

Raw footage, posting, account management and advertising are separate unless the proposal includes them. A filming booking alone does not mean someone will manage your social accounts afterwards.

## How many videos can one filming day produce?

There is no useful fixed answer before the brief is known. A set of short answers in one location takes different preparation from several demonstrations, speakers or locations. Editing also depends on length, subtitles, graphics and the number of versions.

Ask for an agreed finished video count rather than an open promise to film as much as possible. ## What affects the cost and delivery date?

Filming time, locations, people, edited video count and production requirements determine the quote. Photography or a larger interview setup can change the work involved. For a deadline, share the date the content must be published, so filming, review and changes can be planned backwards from it.
