Prepare for a first business video shoot by deciding who needs to watch, what they need to understand and what they should do next. Then confirm the location, people, filming permissions and finished video list with the producer. You do not need a perfect script to start; a clear question and an honest answer are more useful than a memorised sales pitch.

## A week before: define the message

Choose a single customer problem for each short video. Write a few prompts, not paragraphs to memorise. Ask your team which questions come up in real conversations. For technical or regulated subjects, identify who will check claims and approvals before filming.

## Two days before: prepare the location

Choose a room away from noisy equipment and interruptions. Check whether the business can pause music, arrange access and make a parking or loading plan. Tidy distracting items that are not part of the story, while keeping the setting representative of the real work. Tell the producer about narrow spaces, access restrictions and other practical constraints.

## On the day: prepare people and objects

Tell participants when they are needed and what they will be asked. Gather products, uniforms or equipment shown in the brief. Confirm consent and any customer or employee permissions before filming. Build in time for setup, short breaks and retakes instead of scheduling every minute.

## After filming: agree the review process

Decide who will collect feedback, what the included revision rounds cover and when the material is needed. Check subtitles, factual statements, brand names and contact details in the draft. ## Choosing the right production

The preparation differs for [short-form videos](/short-form-video-production-dublin/), a [corporate interview](/corporate-video-production-dublin/) and [event coverage](/event-videographer-dublin/): prioritise the people and moments that matter for your format.
