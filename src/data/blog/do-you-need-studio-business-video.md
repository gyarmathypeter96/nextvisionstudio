No: a professional business video does not always need a studio. An office, workshop or customer-approved location can give viewers useful context when sound, light and access are manageable. A studio can be a better choice when the project needs a controlled background, specialist setup or freedom from interruptions.

## When filming at your business works

A real workplace can show the people, equipment and process behind a service. Choose a quiet area with enough room for the camera, lighting and the speaker. Check whether work can continue safely and whether confidential documents, screens or customers may appear in shot.

## When a studio is worth considering

A controlled setting can help when the brief requires a consistent backdrop, several speakers in the same setup or lighting that is difficult to achieve on site. Studio hire, travel and any specialist crew should be itemised in the quote; do not assume a venue is included.

## Check sound before aesthetics

Traffic, refrigeration, ventilation and music can make an attractive room difficult to record in. Ask whether machinery can be paused and whether there is a quieter alternative. The producer can advise on the setup after understanding the room and subject.

## Choose according to the finished video

A practical demonstration may benefit from its real environment; a focused interview may need a quiet neutral room. Agree permissions, filming time, edits and delivery first. Then choose the setting that helps the viewer understand the subject rather than hiring space by default.
