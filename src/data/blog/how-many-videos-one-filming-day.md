There is no reliable fixed number of short videos from one filming day. A focused set of answers from one speaker in one location can produce more usable material than a day involving several people, locations and demonstrations. Agree the finished deliverables before booking, then build the shot list backwards from them.

## Start with the finished videos

List the questions each video should answer, where it will appear and whether it needs a separate introduction or call to action. Four distinct customer questions are a clearer brief than a request for “lots of content”. One longer interview can also support shorter clips, but only if the answers work when separated from their original context.

## What limits the number you can produce?

Allow for setup, sound checks, lighting, retakes, moving between rooms and the time needed to make people comfortable. A product demonstration may need resets between takes. An event cannot be repeated for the camera. If multiple people need approval before appearing, settle that before filming day.

## Filming is only part of the work

Every finished edit needs selection, assembly and review. Captions, graphics, versions in different aspect ratios, music clearance and extra revisions add work even when the footage comes from one session. Ask for a written deliverable list rather than assuming every take becomes a finished video.
