## Start with the job the film must do

A customer needs to understand a service; a new employee needs to learn a process; a potential recruit wants to meet the team. These briefs can all use interviews, but the questions, supporting footage and review process are different.

Write one sentence describing the intended viewer and the action you want them to take. Then decide where the film will appear. A website explanation can need a horizontal version, while supporting social clips need their own openings and captions. Our [guide to planning a business video](/blog/business-video-production-planning-dublin/) helps turn that decision into a shoot plan.

## What changes a corporate video quote?

**Preparation:** interview questions, script development, shot lists and factual review all take work before filming. Decide who supplies technical information and who signs it off.

**People and locations:** one prepared speaker in a quiet room is a different production from several teams across multiple premises. Share room access, background noise and the time each person can give.

**Finished edits:** specify the main film, its approximate length, shorter versions, subtitles and any graphics. A filming day does not automatically include an unlimited number of edits.

**Review:** agree the first-edit date, revision rounds and one person to consolidate feedback. A change in the agreed message can need more work than a correction to a title.
