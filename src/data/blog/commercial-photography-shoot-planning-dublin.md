A commercial photography shoot should not begin with a camera. It should begin with a list of places where the images need to work.

Your website may need a wide hero image. Google Business Profile needs clear, credible photographs of the business. LinkedIn may need team portraits. A campaign may need square, vertical and horizontal versions of the same idea.

When those uses are agreed first, one well-planned shoot can create a useful image library instead of a folder of attractive photographs that do not fit anywhere.

This guide explains how to plan a commercial photography shoot for a Dublin business, from the first brief to final delivery.

## Start with the business goal

The photographer needs to understand what the images are meant to change.

Common goals include:

- making a new website feel more credible;
- launching a product or service;
- showing the people behind the business;
- replacing inconsistent phone photographs;
- creating campaign assets for social media and advertising;
- documenting an event, venue or customer experience.

Choose one primary goal, even if the shoot will support several channels. It gives the photography a clear standard. A product launch needs different images from a recruitment campaign, even when both feature the same team.

## List every place the photographs will appear

Before writing the shot list, make a simple channel list.

For a typical Dublin business, that might include:

- website homepage and service pages;
- Google Business Profile;
- LinkedIn company and team profiles;
- Instagram, Facebook or TikTok covers;
- digital advertisements;
- sales presentations and proposals;
- printed brochures, menus or signage;
- press and partnership material.

Each channel has its own shape and space requirements. Website headlines often sit beside or over an image, so the composition needs room for text. A vertical Reel cover needs the subject in a safe central area. A team photograph may need both a wide group version and individual crops.

Tell the photographer where copy, buttons or logos will sit. This small detail prevents expensive cropping problems later.

## Build a shot list around customer questions

A useful shot list is not just a list of objects. It should answer the questions a potential customer has before contacting you.

For a service business, customers may want to see:

- who will arrive or manage the work;
- what the process looks like;
- what equipment or materials are used;
- what the finished result looks like;
- whether the business feels professional and established;
- what makes the service different.

Turn those questions into photographs. A wide workplace image establishes context. A close detail proves care. A natural team photograph builds familiarity. A before-and-after pair makes the result easy to understand.

Aim for a mix of wide, medium and close images. Repeating the same framing creates a gallery, but not a flexible content library.

## Decide between studio and location photography

Commercial studio photography gives control over light, background and consistency. It is well suited to products, portraits and advertising images that need a clean visual standard.

Location photography shows the real environment. It can make a service, team or hospitality business feel more believable because customers see where and how the work happens.

Some projects benefit from both. Products can be photographed cleanly first, then placed in a real setting for lifestyle and campaign images.

The choice should follow the final use:

- Choose a studio-style setup when consistency and product detail matter most.
- Choose a real location when the space, people or process helps build trust.
- Combine both when a campaign needs clean sales images and more human brand content.

If the location is customer-facing, plan the quietest time of day. Dublin traffic, deliveries, changing daylight and public access can all affect the schedule.

## Prepare people, products and spaces

Most avoidable shoot problems happen before the photographer arrives.

For people, confirm who is taking part, what they should wear and when they are needed. Avoid asking a full team to wait through product setups that do not involve them. Share a simple clothing guide so colours and patterns work together without making everyone look identical.

For products, prepare clean final examples and backups where possible. Remove temporary labels, price stickers and damaged packaging. If several products are being photographed, organise them in the order of the shot list.

For spaces, remove clutter that does not support the story. Check windows, screens, cables, bins, paperwork and branded material. A photographer can improve composition, but a tidy, prepared location saves time and keeps the images honest.

## Plan for Dublin light and weather

Weather does not need to ruin a shoot, but it does need a backup plan.

For exterior photography, agree what happens if conditions are unsuitable. Can the schedule move indoors? Is there a covered alternative? Which images genuinely depend on dry weather?

For interiors, visit or share photographs of the location in advance. Large windows can create beautiful natural light, but the direction and strength change through the day. Mixed office lighting may also need to be controlled.

If a public location, rented studio or managed building is involved, confirm access and permissions before the shoot. Do not assume a camera setup will be treated the same as an ordinary visitor.

## Agree the image formats before the shoot

Ask what will be delivered, not only how many photographs.

A clear delivery plan can include:

- high-resolution files for print;
- web-optimised files for fast loading;
- horizontal, square and vertical crops;
- transparent or clean-background product images where required;
- file naming that makes the library easy to search;
- colour and retouching expectations;
- agreed usage rights.

Usage matters because a photograph for an organic social post may have a different commercial purpose from an image used in a national advertising campaign. Confirm the planned use before signing off the scope.

## Create a library, not a single campaign

The most efficient commercial shoots create both immediate campaign assets and evergreen brand images.

Alongside the priority photographs, capture:

- clean portraits;
- workplace details;
- hands and process shots;
- product close-ups;
- neutral images with space for text;
- vertical options for social content;
- seasonal details only when they are intentional.

Evergreen photographs give the team something credible to use between larger campaigns. They also reduce the temptation to fill important pages with generic stock images.

## Common commercial photography mistakes

Avoid these problems before the shoot begins:

1. Booking a photographer without a final-use list.
2. Creating only horizontal images when most publishing is mobile.
3. Photographing every person in the same pose and location.
4. Forgetting space for website text and advertising copy.
5. Leaving products, rooms or clothing decisions until the shoot day.
6. Expecting one image to work for every format.
7. Ignoring usage rights and delivery specifications.

A short planning call and a clear shot list solve most of them.

## A simple commercial photography brief

You do not need a long creative document. Send the photographer:

- the business goal;
- the audience;
- the pages and channels using the images;
- the people, products and locations involved;
- three to five visual references;
- the priority shot list;
- the required formats;
- the preferred date and deadline;
- any access, permission or brand restrictions.

The photographer can then challenge gaps, group similar setups and recommend what is realistic in the available time.

## Choose a photographer who plans the use, not just the look

Portfolio quality matters, but the working process matters too. Ask how the photographer will prepare the shot list, manage the schedule and deliver files for different channels.

A good commercial photographer should be able to explain why an image is being created and where it will work. That makes the shoot easier to approve internally and far more useful after delivery.
