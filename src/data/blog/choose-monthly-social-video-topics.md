Choose monthly video topics from questions your customers actually ask, then match each answer to a useful action. A small set of clear topics is easier to film and review than a calendar filled with unrelated trends. Start with the conversations your team already has when a potential customer is deciding whether to enquire.

## Collect questions from real conversations

Ask the people answering the phone, sending quotes or meeting customers what comes up repeatedly. Look at questions about how a service works, what is included, preparation and the next step. Remove private details and check technical or regulated claims with the person responsible.

## Choose different stages of the decision

One video can explain a problem, another can show how a service is carried out, and a third can answer what happens after an enquiry. Use a real customer example only with permission and avoid inventing results. The [VentSolve project](/case-studies/ventsolve-content-production/) describes a mix of brand, educational and internal video work.

## Build a one-line brief for each video

Write the audience, question, short answer, supporting footage and next step. Note the person who can approve factual details. If one answer needs a lengthy explanation, consider a longer website video and a short version for social media.

## Make the calendar fit your capacity

Group topics that use the same person, location or demonstration into one filming session. Agree the number of edits, captions, formats and approval dates before the shoot. Review which questions lead to useful conversations, alongside views and engagement; a view by itself is not an enquiry.
