Put a business video where it answers the question a visitor has at that point on your site. A short introduction can help explain a service; a detailed demonstration can support a specific product or service page. Add a clear next step and keep the essential information available as text too.

## Match the video to the page

A service page can explain who the service is for, what happens and how to enquire. A project page can show the brief and the finished work. The [Balbriggan Braces case study](/case-studies/balbriggan-braces-dental-videos/) describes an educational video collection; it is an example of the work rather than a promise of a business result.

## Make it easy to watch

Use a descriptive thumbnail, an obvious play control and captions where the video contains speech. Avoid relying on autoplay with sound. On phones, check that the player fits the screen and that viewers can pause, change volume or enlarge it.

## Support the video with useful text

A concise summary helps someone decide whether to watch and gives context to visitors who prefer reading. Include relevant details, conditions and an accessible way to contact the business. If a claim needs a source or qualification, provide it in the page content.

## Measure meaningful actions

Track visits and video interactions alongside enquiries, with appropriate consent and a clear measurement setup. An increase in plays alone does not prove more customers. Compare the same page and time window before and after a change, while recognising that small visitor numbers make results uncertain.
